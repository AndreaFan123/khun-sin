# Taiwan Marine Conservation Content Website Competitive Analysis

**Status:** Directional market and editorial analysis  
**Research date:** 2026-10-17  
**Geographic scope:** Taiwan  
**Category scope:** Marine conservation content, with particular attention to cetaceans and sea turtles  
**Primary use:** Positioning the Khun-Sin international cetacean and sea turtle content MVP  

## Executive Summary

Taiwan already has a substantial marine-conservation content ecosystem, but it is fragmented by organisational mission. The main websites do not compete as interchangeable media products: they compete for the same limited reader attention through different jobs, including field-research communication, rescue guidance, citizen science, behaviour change, policy advocacy, journalism, environmental education, and fundraising.

The strongest existing positions are:

- **Kuroshio Ocean Education Foundation:** Taiwan cetacean field research and place-based storytelling;
- **Taiwan Cetacean Society:** stranding rescue guidance, volunteering, education, and organisational participation;
- **TurtleSpot Taiwan / TurtleFinder:** sea turtle citizen science, individual identification, sightings, and participation;
- **RE-THINK:** marine debris and recycling translated into accessible, designed, participatory experiences;
- **Greenpeace Taiwan:** campaign-led policy advocacy with petitions and mobilisation;
- **Environmental Information Center:** news, policy reporting, investigation, and long-form environmental features;
- **HiiN Studio:** action-oriented environmental education, beach clean-ups, workshops, and organisational services;
- **Ocean Conservation Administration:** official information, publications, rescue systems, education, projects, and public-sector achievements.

The most defensible opening for Khun-Sin is not to become another general ocean-news site, plastic-reduction campaign, domestic citizen-science database, or rescue organisation. The clearest whitespace is:

> A source-transparent, Traditional-Chinese-first editorial service that monitors international cetacean and sea turtle conservation, explains the evidence in accessible language, and adds an honest Taiwan relevance layer, with an adapted English version.

That position complements rather than imitates the strongest Taiwanese organisations. Its advantage would come from consistent international monitoring, cross-source verification, bilingual fact cards, explicit uncertainty, and a predictable weekly format.

## Research Question

How do existing Taiwan-based marine-conservation websites prefer to frame and package their content, and which editorial position remains meaningfully under-served for Khun-Sin?

## Important Interpretation Boundary

This report analyses **content supply preference**: what topics, formats, and calls to action each website visibly prioritises.

It does **not** establish audience preference or market share. Public web pages do not provide comparable reach, completion, retention, save, share, click, donation, or conversion data. A site publishing many rescue stories does not prove that rescue stories perform best. Any claim about reader demand must be tested with Khun-Sin's own analytics or obtained directly from the organisations.

The review is directional rather than an exhaustive quantitative content census. It uses current official website navigation, landing pages, recent or featured content, project pages, and public calls to action available on 2026-10-17. Social-only activity may be underrepresented.

## Competitive Set

The sample deliberately includes four kinds of attention competitors:

1. **Specialist NGOs and projects** — direct thematic overlap with cetaceans, sea turtles, or marine debris;
2. **Advocacy and media organisations** — compete for conservation-minded readers and search discovery;
3. **Education and action providers** — turn marine interest into courses, clean-ups, workshops, or citizen participation;
4. **Government platforms** — authoritative reference and public education benchmarks, even though they are not commercial competitors.

## Content Preference Archetypes

### 1. Field Evidence and Place-Based Storytelling

**Representative:** Kuroshio Ocean Education Foundation

Kuroshio's homepage prominently features sperm whale Photo-ID, cetacean captivity and retirement questions, the Ocean Oasis East Coast whale programme, rare whale observations, scar identification, and long-term sighting data. Its content preference is not generic animal trivia: it connects original observation, named projects, eastern Taiwan, research history, and organisational values. Animal welfare and ethical judgement also appear as editorial themes. [Official site](https://www.kuroshio.org.tw/newsite/)

**Typical reader promise:** Follow people who know this sea and have observed these animals over time.

**Content strengths:**

- unique first-party evidence;
- geographic and emotional specificity;
- strong photography and charismatic species;
- science interpreted through human field experience;
- natural bridge to donation and programme support.

**Implication for Khun-Sin:** Do not imitate Kuroshio's domestic field authority. Khun-Sin should cite and contextualise first-party organisations rather than presenting itself as a field-research body.

### 2. Rescue Utility and Organisational Participation

**Representative:** Taiwan Cetacean Society

The Taiwan Cetacean Society organises its public website around stranding rescue, emergency procedures, rehabilitation volunteer information, historical stranding locations, education, public-benefit whale watching, organisational reports, and donations. It prominently provides both its rescue number and the Coast Guard 118 hotline. [Official site](https://www.whale.org.tw/)

**Typical reader promise:** Know what to do, reach the rescue network, or become part of it.

**Content strengths:**

- high practical relevance at the moment of need;
- direct connection to rescue and rehabilitation;
- clear volunteer and educational pathways;
- operational rather than purely symbolic conservation.

**Implication for Khun-Sin:** Rescue guidance must link to authoritative current channels and must never imply that Khun-Sin is an official intake or response organisation. Khun-Sin can explain rescue systems internationally, but should not compete on operational authority.

### 3. Citizen Science and Individual-Animal Attachment

**Representatives:** TurtleSpot Taiwan and TurtleFinder

TurtleSpot invites divers to submit sea turtle sightings and images, uses facial-scale patterns and other characteristics for individual identification, and builds a Taiwan turtle registry. Its terms explicitly retain photographers' rights and describe CC BY-NC conditions for contributed data and images. [TurtleSpot overview and terms](https://turtlespottw.org/about)

TurtleFinder extends this pattern into an official 2026 platform with species education, sighting reports, sighting records, project milestones, and an explicit invitation to become a marine citizen scientist. [TurtleFinder](https://turtlefinder.oca.gov.tw/) [Project timeline](https://turtlefinder.oca.gov.tw/timeline)

**Typical reader promise:** Your observation can identify an individual animal and contribute to real knowledge.

**Content strengths:**

- active participation rather than passive reading;
- memorable individual animals;
- visible link between images, data, research, and conservation;
- repeat use for divers and committed participants.

**Implication for Khun-Sin:** Do not build a competing sighting database as a content MVP. A better role is to explain international citizen-science methods, highlight outcomes, and direct eligible Taiwan observations to the established platforms.

### 4. Designed Behaviour Change

**Representative:** RE-THINK

RE-THINK frames environmental problems through everyday behaviour and participation. Its website foregrounds clean-ups, school education, recycling, interactive products such as the Marine Debris Illustrated Guide and Recycling Encyclopedia, public advocacy, individual participation, and corporate collaboration. It explicitly positions its work as making environmental action part of daily life. [Official site](https://rethinktw.org/) [Marine Debris Illustrated Guide](https://oceantrash.rethinktw.org/zh-TW/about)

**Typical reader promise:** Environmental problems can be understood and acted on through accessible, interesting experiences.

**Content strengths:**

- strong visual and interaction design;
- plain-language translation of systems;
- low-friction actions;
- cross-sector partnerships and educational products;
- clear impact and donation storytelling.

**Implication for Khun-Sin:** Generic plastic-reduction advice is crowded and would be weak differentiation. Khun-Sin should borrow the clarity and interaction quality, but keep its subject advantage in cetaceans, sea turtles, evidence, and international comparison.

### 5. Campaign and Policy Mobilisation

**Representative:** Greenpeace Taiwan

Greenpeace's ocean hub combines international and Taiwan policy, fisheries, marine protected areas, treaties, investigations, campaign victories, petitions, donations, and daily action commitments. The site gives the reader a defined opponent, policy demand, deadline, or action route. [Taiwan ocean hub](https://www.greenpeace.org/taiwan/%E6%B5%B7%E6%B4%8B/) [Marine protected area campaign](https://www.greenpeace.org/taiwan/%E6%B5%B7%E6%B4%8B/%E6%93%B4%E5%A4%A7%E8%87%BA%E7%81%A3%E6%B5%B7%E6%B4%8B%E4%BF%9D%E8%AD%B7%E5%8D%80/)

**Typical reader promise:** Understand the threat, join a collective demand, and help produce policy change.

**Content strengths:**

- high urgency and strong headlines;
- clear relationship between evidence and demand;
- international campaign resources localised for Taiwan;
- strong conversion design.

**Implication for Khun-Sin:** Khun-Sin should clearly label when it is explaining evidence, presenting an organisation's campaign position, or making its own recommendation. It should not adopt campaign certainty by default.

### 6. Journalism, Policy Context, and Deep Explanation

**Representative:** Environmental Information Center

The Environmental Information Center is a broad nonprofit environmental newsroom. Its marine coverage includes international conference reporting, tourism, marine policy, sea turtle stranding data, offshore wind and cetacean observers, and long-form special packages. Its value comes from reporting, expert interviews, institutional context, and historical follow-up rather than a single species identity. [About the newsroom](https://www.e-info.org.tw/about) [Deep features](https://www.e-info.org.tw/feature)

**Typical reader promise:** Understand the actors, evidence, conflict, and policy behind an environmental issue.

**Content strengths:**

- journalistic depth and archive;
- policy and stakeholder complexity;
- Taiwan and international reporting;
- search-oriented evergreen special features;
- ability to revisit issues over multiple articles.

**Implication for Khun-Sin:** Do not attempt to out-report a full environmental newsroom. Khun-Sin can win through narrower monitoring, predictable species focus, shorter evidence cards, bilingual adaptation, and clearer recurring formats.

### 7. Experiential Education and Service-Led Action

**Representative:** HiiN Studio

HiiN's public offer centres on clean-ups, talks, DIY workshops, coastal trips, reusable tableware, community planning, and corporate environmental programmes. Its course examples connect sea turtles to plastic pollution, seafood choices to ocean conservation, and games to common-resource problems. [Official site](https://hiinstudio.com/) [Programme invitations](https://hiinstudio.com/joinhiin/) [Lecture topics](https://hiinstudio.com/joinhiin/lecture/)

**Typical reader promise:** Learn by doing, then bring the activity to your school, organisation, or community.

**Content strengths:**

- tangible participation;
- education adapted across age groups;
- offline and organisational conversion;
- marine issues connected to daily consumption.

**Observed limitation:** The website's visible news archive is weighted toward earlier activity reports, while the enduring proposition is services and events rather than a continuously updated public editorial archive.

**Implication for Khun-Sin:** Khun-Sin's initial advantage should be consistent editorial publishing, not competing event operations. Later partnerships could convert strong articles into talks or educational modules.

### 8. Official Education, Systems, and Public Records

**Representative:** Ocean Conservation Administration

The Ocean Conservation Administration operates multiple public surfaces: rescue information, stranding records, species and citizen-science systems, project and ESG matching, government news, educational pages, and the *Ocean Waves* conservation quarterly. The quarterly spans citizen science, marine culture, pollution response, circular economy, seabirds, international exchange, education, and cross-sector stories. [Quarterly archive](https://www.oca.gov.tw/ch/home.jsp?id=390&parentpath=0%2C298%2C386) [Marine protected wildlife system](https://mum.oca.gov.tw/) [Rescue network](https://www.oca.gov.tw/ch/home.jsp?id=185&parentpath=0)

**Typical reader promise:** Obtain official information, access public systems, and understand government-supported conservation work.

**Content strengths:**

- authority and official system ownership;
- broad subject and geographic coverage;
- access to public programmes, records, and statistics;
- ability to connect education to administration.

**Implication for Khun-Sin:** Government sources should anchor factual verification, but Khun-Sin can add value by creating coherent reader journeys across otherwise separate systems and documents.

## What Taiwan's Marine Content Supply Currently Favours

Across the reviewed sites, six preferences repeatedly appear.

### 1. Charismatic animals and rescue narratives

Whales, dolphins, and sea turtles are used as the emotional entry point. Rescue, injury, stranding, individual identity, and rare sightings produce immediate human interest.

### 2. Marine debris as the default everyday issue

Plastic, fishing gear, beach litter, recycling, and clean-ups dominate the bridge between marine conservation and daily behaviour. This is accessible and actionable, but also crowded.

### 3. Participation over passive awareness

Successful propositions commonly offer a concrete next step: report a sighting, join a clean-up, attend a lecture, sign a petition, donate, volunteer, choose seafood differently, or use an official service.

### 4. Taiwan locality and named places

East Coast cetaceans, Xiaoliuqiu turtles, Penghu community work, specific beaches, offshore wind zones, fishing ports, and local protected areas give the content credibility and emotional weight.

### 5. Organisational mission determines the story

Research organisations tell field stories, rescue organisations teach response, advocacy organisations demand policy, media organisations investigate conflict, and education providers convert knowledge into activities. Few sites regularly compare multiple international organisations from a neutral, species-focused editorial layer.

### 6. Traditional Chinese dominates; bilingual depth is uneven

English labels, translation widgets, or isolated English features exist, but a consistent article-level Traditional Chinese and English mirror is not the default across the category. This creates an opportunity, but only if the English version demonstrates actual value relative to its production cost.

## Content Whitespace

### Strongest opportunity: international evidence, interpreted for Taiwan

No reviewed site clearly owns a weekly, source-transparent service that:

- monitors international cetacean and sea turtle conservation sources;
- distinguishes NGO claims, scientific evidence, and official policy;
- explains uncertainty and research method;
- gives the issue an honest Taiwan context;
- maintains aligned Traditional Chinese and English versions;
- preserves source and rights provenance for every item.

Environmental Information Center sometimes covers international marine affairs and Greenpeace localises international campaigns, but neither is narrowly structured around a recurring cetacean-and-sea-turtle editorial product.

### Secondary opportunities

- **Research-method explainers:** Photo-ID, acoustic monitoring, tagging, pathology, drone sampling, bycatch estimation, and population modelling;
- **System comparisons:** How different countries handle sightings, strandings, rehabilitation, fisheries interaction, or protected areas;
- **Evidence updates:** What changed since an earlier conservation claim, rescue, policy, or study;
- **Myth versus evidence:** Common assumptions separated from established findings and uncertainty;
- **Case lifecycle:** Observation → verification → response → research → policy, without exposing sensitive locations;
- **International-to-Taiwan action bridge:** A realistic local action or official resource, not a generic “protect the ocean” ending.

## Positions Khun-Sin Should Avoid

- **General environmental newsroom:** Environmental Information Center has greater breadth and reporting capacity.
- **Domestic cetacean field authority:** Kuroshio owns long-term first-party observation and East Coast identity.
- **Rescue operator:** Taiwan Cetacean Society and MARN participants hold operational legitimacy.
- **Sea turtle sighting database:** TurtleSpot and TurtleFinder already provide the participation and data pathway.
- **Generic plastic and recycling education:** RE-THINK has stronger products and behaviour-change recognition.
- **Petition-first advocacy:** Greenpeace has campaign scale, legal and policy teams, and conversion infrastructure.
- **Clean-up and workshop provider:** HiiN and other local organisations already turn education into physical activities.
- **Government information mirror:** Repeating official announcements without editorial interpretation creates little value.

## Recommended Khun-Sin Editorial Position

### Positioning statement

> Khun-Sin helps Taiwan readers understand what international cetacean and sea turtle conservation evidence means, where it is uncertain, and how it connects—or does not connect—to Taiwan.

### Editorial promise

Every story should provide:

1. **The event or finding** — what happened and who reported it;
2. **The evidence** — what is known, how it was measured, and what remains uncertain;
3. **The Taiwan lens** — a genuine comparison, local relevance, or explicit statement that no direct connection is established;
4. **The next step** — an official resource, further reading, or proportionate action;
5. **The provenance** — primary source, verification source, date, and media rights basis.

### Recommended weekly format

| Weekly slot | Format | Competitive purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Cetacean brief | One international development in five-minute form | More focused and regular than a broad newsroom |
| Sea turtle evidence card | One finding, method, or case with a visual explanation | Educational without duplicating Taiwan's sighting platform |
| Taiwan connection | Compare a shared threat, policy, research method, or response system | Adds local value beyond translation |

### Recommended tone

- curious rather than alarmist;
- emotionally engaging without exploiting animal suffering;
- direct about uncertainty;
- respectful of field and rescue organisations;
- action-oriented without manufacturing urgency;
- accessible to general readers while retaining links to original evidence.

## Product and UX Implications

The competitive analysis supports the five-collection workbench already proposed in the [international content MVP](../ideas/international-cetacean-sea-turtle-content-mvp.md), but adds several requirements:

- tag every item by editorial archetype: rescue, research, policy, citizen science, behaviour, or community;
- track whether the story duplicates a strong Taiwan provider's existing role;
- require a `taiwan_connection_strength` field such as `direct`, `comparable`, `context_only`, or `none`;
- require an `evidence_type` field such as official record, peer-reviewed study, NGO field report, press release, or reported testimony;
- separate `reader_action` from `organisation_conversion`, so a donation request is not mistaken for the only possible action;
- record sensitive-location and animal-welfare checks;
- preserve a public-facing source list instead of hiding research behind a generic “source” label;
- measure English-version use separately from Traditional Chinese performance.

## Suggested Validation Plan

### Phase 1: Content prototype

Produce nine unpublished sample packages over three weeks:

- three cetacean briefs;
- three sea turtle evidence cards;
- three Taiwan-connection explainers.

Use at least five different international primary sources and avoid reusing source imagery unless rights are verified.

### Phase 2: Reader review

Show the prototypes to 6–10 Taiwan readers with mixed conservation familiarity. Ask them to identify:

- what they learned;
- which claim they trust and why;
- whether the Taiwan connection felt useful or forced;
- what they would save, share, or follow;
- whether the English version serves any actual job;
- whether they know what to do next.

### Phase 3: Eight-week live MVP

Publish the three recurring weekly formats and compare:

- completion or engaged reading where available;
- saves and shares;
- original-source clicks;
- return visits;
- corrections and reader questions;
- production time;
- source yield;
- rights-related blockers.

## Decision Rules

- **Proceed:** readers repeatedly value the international evidence and Taiwan explanation, and production remains sustainable.
- **Narrow:** one species or one format clearly produces stronger value; reduce the weaker area rather than filling a quota.
- **Change the English strategy:** the English version has no observable use relative to its cost.
- **Partner instead of build:** the strongest reader need is already served by a Taiwan organisation with operational or scientific authority.
- **Stop:** the output is perceived as translated NGO news, lacks a distinct Taiwan layer, or cannot be produced with reliable rights and verification.

## Limitations

- The audit does not include private analytics, social-platform reach, newsletter performance, donation conversion, or organisational interviews.
- Website update frequency may not reflect activity on Facebook, Instagram, YouTube, LINE, events, or offline programmes.
- Search-engine and crawler visibility can omit JavaScript-rendered content or misrepresent publication recency.
- The analysis classifies visible editorial supply; it does not rank organisational conservation impact.
- Content and site structures can change after the research date and should be rechecked before implementation or partnership outreach.
- Source inclusion does not imply permission to scrape, translate, reproduce, or reuse media. Rights must be reviewed separately before any content pipeline is activated.
