# Three-week editorial experiment review

**Observation window:** 2026-W43 to 2026-W45  
**Status:** Completed as an unpublished structural prototype  
**Decision boundary:** This review evaluates source yield, workflow repeatability, and rights friction. It does not establish reader demand, editorial sustainability, or publication readiness.

## Outcome

The prototype produced nine structured bilingual packages over three simulated weekly cycles:

- three cetacean packages;
- three sea-turtle packages;
- three shared or Taiwan-connection packages;
- six international primary source organisations represented across the selected set;
- no source media copied into the repository.

The same registry → candidate → fact card → Traditional Chinese draft → English adaptation → review → performance-record structure worked across rescue, bycatch, spatial priorities, population assessment, field monitoring, and citizen science. This demonstrates structural repeatability. It does not demonstrate how long a human editor would take or whether readers would value the output.

## What the cycles revealed

| Question | Evidence from the prototype | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| Can the source set supply three differentiated topics per week? | Nine selected candidates across six international organisations and Taiwan OCA | Provisionally yes for three weeks; longer-term yield is untested |
| Can one schema cover different story types? | All nine packages pass the same package validator | Yes at the document-structure level |
| Can the project publish from these sources safely today? | Seven packages are blocked; two are only review-ready | No; rights and human approval remain material gates |
| Is source media necessary for the format? | All packages use `source_media_used: none` | No for drafting; publication still needs an original or clearly licensed visual plan |
| Has audience demand been validated? | No packages were published or reviewed by target readers | No |
| Has editorial workload been validated? | `production_minutes` is empty for all records | No |

## Rights finding

The easiest current publication base is official government material with an explicit reuse policy, such as NOAA-created public-domain text and OCA material covered by Taiwan Government Data Open Licence 1.0, subject to item-level exceptions and media credits.

IWC, WDC, Sea Turtle Conservancy, IMMA, and IUCN/MTSG introduce permission, non-commercial, commercial-use, or terms-applicability questions. Original paraphrasing and source links reduce copying risk but do not replace a rights decision for a free/paid content product. The pipeline therefore fails closed.

This suggests a practical source portfolio:

1. Use rights-clear government and open-data sources as the dependable publishing base.
2. Use restricted NGO and expert-network sources for discovery and internal fact extraction.
3. Seek written partnership or reuse terms only for sources that repeatedly produce high-value topics.
4. Store no full articles and copy no source media by default.

## Editorial finding

The strongest information gain came from explaining the mechanism behind a conservation claim:

- a mitigation device requires training, inspection, repair, and enforcement;
- a scientific priority area is not automatically a legal protected area;
- a species label is less precise than a regional population assessment;
- a non-detection becomes more useful when observation effort and conditions are recorded;
- structured reporting is different from public wildlife handling or emergency response.

That pattern is a clearer differentiator than translated conservation news. It also supports the stated Taiwan whitespace: explain international cetacean and sea-turtle evidence in Traditional Chinese, mark uncertainty, and state whether the Taiwan connection is direct, comparable, contextual, or absent.

## What remains unverified

- Whether 6–10 target readers understand, save, share, or return for these formats.
- Whether a human editor can complete three packages per week at acceptable cost.
- Whether subject specialists can review the Taiwan comparison reliably.
- Whether restricted organisations will grant commercial or partnership permission.
- Whether original visual production is affordable and repeatable.
- Whether the content leads to source clicks, reporting literacy, membership, donations, or another defined outcome.

## Recommendation

Do not build a custom content backend yet. Pause synthetic production at nine packages and run two human validations:

1. **Reader review:** show a representative set to 6–10 intended readers. Test comprehension, trust, Taiwan relevance, desired depth, and willingness to save or share.
2. **Editor timing:** have a human editor produce or revise one complete week while recording minutes for discovery, rights review, fact checking, Traditional Chinese editing, English adaptation, subject review, and visual preparation.

After those tests, choose one of three paths:

- **Continue manually** if reader value is clear and workload is manageable.
- **Automate repeated clerical steps** if value is clear but source monitoring, deduplication, required-field checks, or performance import repeatedly consume time.
- **Change the editorial proposition** if readers do not value the mechanism-first format, regardless of how well the validator works.

The current evidence supports a source-grounded editorial MVP, not a backend investment or a claim of market demand.
