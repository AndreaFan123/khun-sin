# 談海龜保育，物種名稱還不夠

## Fact card

### Proposed angle

Show why marine-turtle priorities are assessed by regional management units and multiple dimensions rather than by one global species label.

### Central claim

Conservation priority depends on which population and region are being assessed, what threats occur there, and whether local institutions can respond—not only on the species name.

### Confirmed facts

- The IUCN SSC Marine Turtle Specialist Group's Conservation Priorities Portfolio 2.0 assesses marine-turtle Regional Management Units.
- The framework considers population abundance, long- and short-term trends, rookery vulnerability, and genetic diversity.
- It also considers threats including bycatch, consumption, coastal development, pollution and pathogens, and climate change.
- Conservation capacity is assessed alongside biological risk and threats.
- The group reports contributions from about 150 experts in 50 countries.

### Uncertainty and limits

- A global framework does not replace a site-specific or Taiwan-specific assessment.
- Different indicators may have missing or uneven data; this package does not audit the underlying datasets or weighting.
- A priority ranking does not prescribe one intervention or prove that resources were allocated.

### Taiwan connection

The connection is contextual. Taiwan-related decisions need evidence about the relevant nesting, foraging, or migratory populations, their threats, and the capacity of responsible institutions. A global species label cannot answer those questions alone.

### Reader action

When a conservation claim names a sea-turtle species, ask which regional population is meant, what evidence describes its trend, which threat is being measured, and who has the capacity to act.

### Claims not to publish

- Every population of one sea-turtle species faces the same risk.
- A global status automatically identifies Taiwan's highest priority.
- The portfolio proves that a listed intervention will work locally.
- IUCN or MTSG graphics may be reused commercially without permission.

## 繁體中文草稿

「這個海龜物種受到威脅」是重要訊息，卻還不足以決定保育工作的先後順序。同一物種在不同海域的族群，數量趨勢、繁殖地脆弱度、混獲壓力與管理能力可能都不一樣。

IUCN 海龜專家小組的 Conservation Priorities Portfolio 2.0，以區域管理單元整理保育優先性。框架不只看族群數量，也納入長短期趨勢、繁殖地脆弱度、遺傳多樣性，以及混獲、利用、沿岸開發、污染、疾病與氣候變遷等威脅。它還把保育能力放進評估，提醒我們：知道問題在哪裡，不代表當地已有資源處理。

這類框架的價值，是讓討論從「某物種很重要」前進到「哪一個族群、面對哪一種威脅、由誰採取什麼行動」。但全球框架不能直接替代臺灣的在地資料，也不會自動產生預算、執法或成效。

下次看到海龜保育排名，可以先問：評估的是哪個區域族群？資料涵蓋哪些年份？威脅與保育能力是否分開衡量？這些問題比單看物種名稱更接近真正的決策。

## English adaptation

“This sea-turtle species is threatened” is important information, but it is not enough to set conservation priorities. Populations of the same species can face different trends, nesting-site vulnerability, bycatch pressure, and institutional capacity in different regions.

The IUCN Marine Turtle Specialist Group's Conservation Priorities Portfolio 2.0 assesses Regional Management Units. It considers abundance, long- and short-term trends, rookery vulnerability, genetic diversity, multiple threats, and conservation capacity.

The framework moves the question from “Is this species important?” to “Which population faces which threat, and who can respond?” It does not replace Taiwan-specific evidence or automatically create budgets, regulation, enforcement, or outcomes.

When a priority claim appears, ask which regional population it covers, what period the evidence represents, which threat is measured, and whether capacity is assessed separately from need.
