# 沒看到鯨豚，也可能是一筆有用的紀錄

## Fact card

### Proposed angle

Use a Shorewatch event to explain why observation effort and conditions matter alongside sightings.

### Central claim

A citizen-science record becomes more interpretable when it includes where, when, how long, and under what conditions people watched—even if no animal was seen.

### Confirmed facts

- WDC published its September 2026 Big Watch Weekend report on 1 October 2026.
- Shorewatch volunteers conducted surveys during 4–6 September and shared results for World Whale Count Day 2026.
- WDC reported strong winds and rain at some sites, conditions that can reduce visibility and survey effort.
- Shorewatch is a citizen-science programme that records land-based whale and dolphin observations.

### Uncertainty and limits

- The event report is not a population estimate and does not establish a trend.
- A non-detection does not prove that animals were absent; weather, sea state, visibility, site choice, and effort affect detectability.
- This package did not review the full Shorewatch protocol or dataset.

### Taiwan connection

The connection is comparable. Taiwan sighting programmes can also separate “no animal observed” from “no survey completed” by recording effort and conditions, but local protocols need their own scientific review.

### Reader action

When submitting or reading a sighting record, look for date, time, location, observation duration, weather or sea state, viewing method, and whether the observer completed the planned survey.

### Claims not to publish

- No sighting means no cetaceans were present.
- One weekend's observations measure population size or change.
- The Scottish protocol can be copied directly into Taiwan without review.
- WDC text or images may be republished in a commercial service without permission.

## 繁體中文草稿

公民科學最吸引人的畫面，往往是鯨豚現身的瞬間。但對研究而言，「看了多久、在什麼條件下看」和「看到了什麼」同樣重要。

WDC 的 Shorewatch 志工在 2026 年 9 月進行岸上觀察，部分地點遇到強風與降雨。這類條件會影響能見度、海面辨識與實際調查時間。若只留下「有看到」或「沒看到」，後來的人很難判斷兩次紀錄是否真的可比較。

一次沒有發現鯨豚的完整調查，仍能告訴我們觀察地點、時間、持續多久與當時條件。相反地，沒有紀錄調查努力的目擊，雖然可能真實，卻較難用來推論出現頻率。沒有看到也不等於動物不在，只代表在特定方法與條件下沒有被發現。

這個原則可以作為臺灣公民觀察計畫的比較參考。提交或閱讀目擊資料時，可留意日期、時間、位置、觀察時長、海況、天氣與觀察方式是否完整。資料品質不只來自精彩照片，也來自規律留下的背景條件。

## English adaptation

Citizen science is often illustrated by the moment a whale or dolphin appears. For research, however, how long people watched and under what conditions can matter as much as what they saw.

WDC's Shorewatch volunteers conducted land-based surveys in September 2026. Strong winds and rain affected some sites. Those conditions can influence visibility, sea-surface detection, and completed survey effort. A simple “seen” or “not seen” record cannot show whether two observations were comparable.

A completed survey with no detection can still document place, time, duration, and conditions. It does not prove that animals were absent; it reports that none were detected with a particular method under particular conditions.

This is a comparable lesson for Taiwan rather than a protocol to copy. Useful records state when and where observation occurred, how long it lasted, the weather or sea state, the viewing method, and whether the planned survey was completed.
