# 一筆可用的海洋動物紀錄，需要哪些資訊？

## Fact card

### Proposed angle

Turn three field examples into a practical data-quality checklist without asking the public to rescue or handle wildlife.

### Central claim

Citizen reports become more useful when they record standard context—time, place, effort, condition, and safely collected evidence—while keeping observation separate from intervention.

### Confirmed facts

- Taiwan's TurtleFinder asks contributors for time, location, and animal status, and recommends multiple photographic views while maintaining an appropriate distance.
- TurtleFinder uses head-scale patterns for photo identification without capturing the turtle.
- WDC's Shorewatch example shows why weather and completed observation effort affect interpretation of a sighting or non-detection.
- STC's nest-dig description shows that wildlife monitoring can involve a controlled method led by trained personnel.
- Taiwan OCA guidance tells the public not to touch, disturb, feed, chase, or harm marine wildlife and to report injuries, strandings, or harassment through official channels.

### Uncertainty and limits

- The required fields differ between routine sightings, photo identification, bycatch, injured animals, and strandings.
- A photo-identification contribution is not an emergency report and should not delay calling the responsible authority.
- This package does not establish data quality, validation rates, or rescue outcomes for any platform.

### Taiwan connection

The connection is direct because TurtleFinder and OCA provide Taiwan-specific contribution and safety guidance. International examples supply comparison, not authority for local response.

### Reader action

For a routine, safe observation, record time, location, animal condition, observation method, and clear images from an appropriate distance. For injury or stranding, prioritise official reporting and safety instructions over completing a citizen-science entry.

### Claims not to publish

- A public report guarantees rescue or scientific use.
- Contributors should move closer, handle an animal, or reposition it for a better photo.
- TurtleFinder replaces Taiwan's emergency reporting channels.
- International programme content or media can be reused commercially without permission.

## 繁體中文草稿

一張海龜或鯨豚照片可以引起注意，但要成為可判讀的紀錄，還需要回答幾個基本問題：什麼時間、什麼位置、觀察多久、動物狀態如何，以及照片是在什麼距離與方法下取得。

臺灣的 TurtleFinder 要求填寫位置、時間與狀態，並建議在適當距離下拍攝不同角度，利用頭部鱗片特徵辨識個體，不必捕捉海龜。WDC 的岸上鯨豚觀察則提醒我們，天氣、海況與觀察努力會影響「看到」或「沒看到」的解讀。海龜巢穴監測的例子又多了一條界線：有些資料只能由受訓、獲准的人員依程序取得。

因此，好的公民紀錄不是愈靠近、愈介入就愈完整。對一般、安全的觀察，可以記下時間、位置、動物狀態、觀察方式與遠距照片；遇到受傷、擱淺或騷擾事件，則應優先聯絡正式通報管道，依指示保持距離，不要為了補資料而觸碰或移動動物。

不同用途需要不同欄位。照片辨識平台不是緊急救援系統，一筆上傳也不能保證會觸發救援或被研究採用。清楚標示用途與下一步，才不會讓「協助蒐集資料」被誤解成「自行救援」。

## English adaptation

A photograph can draw attention to a turtle, whale, or dolphin, but an interpretable record also needs time, place, observation effort, animal condition, and information about how the evidence was collected.

Taiwan's TurtleFinder requests location, time, and status. It recommends several photographic views from an appropriate distance and uses head-scale patterns to identify turtles without capture. WDC's shore surveys show why weather and effort affect the meaning of a detection or non-detection. A nest-monitoring example adds another boundary: some observations require trained and authorised personnel.

Better citizen data does not mean getting closer or intervening. For a routine, safe observation, record context and take clear images from an appropriate distance. For an injured or stranded animal, prioritise the official reporting channel and safety instructions; do not touch or move wildlife to complete a record.

Different purposes require different fields. A photo-identification platform is not an emergency service, and a submission does not guarantee rescue or research use.
