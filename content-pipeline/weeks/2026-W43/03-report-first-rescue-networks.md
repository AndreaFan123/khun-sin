# 發現擱淺鯨豚或海龜，第一步是通報，不是推回海裡

## Fact card

### Proposed angle

Compare Taiwan and United States public guidance to show why a rescue network begins with accurate reporting and trained responders rather than public handling.

### Central claim

When a whale, dolphin, porpoise, or sea turtle is stranded or injured, an accurate report lets authorised responders assess the case. Untrained handling can harm both the animal and the person.

### Confirmed facts

- Taiwan's Ocean Conservation Administration tells the public to call Coast Guard hotline 118 or the local marine-conservation authority when a protected marine animal is stranded or needs rescue.
- The Taiwan guidance asks the reporter to provide the location, time, and a description of the animal's condition.
- The guidance says not to handle the animal without professional direction.
- Taiwan's Marine Animal Rescue Network coordinates central and local government, the Coast Guard, commissioned response teams, experts, and partner groups.
- NOAA Fisheries also directs the public to report stranded or injured marine mammals and sea turtles to trained local response networks and to keep people and pets back.
- NOAA's detailed guidance says that trained responders assess whether release, rehabilitation, supportive care, or another welfare decision is appropriate.

### Uncertainty and limits

- United States distances, phone numbers, and legal rules do not apply in Taiwan.
- This package does not replace instructions from 118, the local authority, or a responder at the scene.
- Animal condition, weather, tides, access, species, and responder capacity can change the correct action.

### Taiwan connection

The connection is direct. In Taiwan, the public entry point is 118 or the relevant county or city marine-conservation authority. The MARN network, not this content project, decides and coordinates the response.

### Reader action

In Taiwan, report the exact location, time, and visible condition to 118 or the local authority. Keep yourself, other people, and pets away. Follow the responder's instructions. Do not push, pull, feed, transport, or return the animal to the water on your own.

### Claims not to publish

- Every stranded animal should be returned to the sea.
- A photo alone is enough to assess the animal.
- The public should copy United States distance rules in Taiwan.
- Khun-Sin accepts emergency reports, dispatches responders, or represents MARN.

## 繁體中文草稿

在海邊看到擱淺的鯨豚或海龜，許多人的第一個念頭是把牠推回海裡。但對受傷、生病、迷航或已經虛弱的動物來說，移動不一定是救援，也可能增加傷害。

臺灣海洋保育署的公開指引要求民眾先撥打海巡 118，或聯絡所在地的縣市海洋保育主管機關。通報時，盡可能說明發現位置、時間與動物狀況。沒有專業人員指導時，不要自行處置動物。

這個流程背後不是一支單獨的救援隊。海保救援網由中央與地方主管機關、海巡、專業行動小組、研究與照護單位共同分工。通報資訊讓地方聯絡與執行樞紐找到現場，也讓專業人員判斷需要哪一種支援。

美國 NOAA 的公開指引也採相同原則。民眾負責通報、保持距離與協助定位，受訓且具權責的人員負責評估。不同國家的電話、距離與法規不能直接套用，但共同點很清楚：現場熱心不能取代專業判斷。

如果你在臺灣遇到擱淺或受傷的鯨豚、海龜，先做三件事。撥打 118 或通知地方主管機關。提供精確位置、時間與可見狀況。保持安全距離並依接線或救援人員指示行動。不要自行推回海裡、搬運、餵食或解開纏繞物。

Khun-Sin 可以協助解釋通報資訊與制度，但不是官方救援入口，也不會自行派遣隊伍。緊急個案仍應直接撥打 118。

## English adaptation

When people find a stranded whale, dolphin, porpoise, or sea turtle, the instinct to return the animal to the water can be strong. An animal may be injured, ill, disoriented, or too weak to survive another attempt. Moving it without an assessment can make the situation worse.

Taiwan's Ocean Conservation Administration tells the public to call the Coast Guard at 118 or the relevant local marine-conservation authority. Reporters should provide the location, time, and visible condition. The public should not handle the animal without professional direction.

The call activates a network rather than a single rescue team. Taiwan's Marine Animal Rescue Network links central and local authorities, the Coast Guard, professional response teams, researchers, and care organisations. The report helps the responsible local unit find the animal and request the right expertise.

NOAA guidance in the United States follows the same operating principle. The public reports the animal and keeps people and pets back. Trained responders assess the animal and decide the appropriate action. United States distances, phone numbers, and legal rules do not apply in Taiwan, but both systems separate public observation from authorised handling.

In Taiwan, call 118 or the local authority, give a precise location and time, describe what you can see, and follow the responder's instructions. Do not push, pull, feed, transport, or disentangle the animal yourself.

Khun-Sin can explain the reporting system. It is not an official reporting channel and does not dispatch rescue teams.
