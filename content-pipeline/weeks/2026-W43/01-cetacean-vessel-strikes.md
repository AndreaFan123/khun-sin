# 船隻撞擊鯨豚，偵測科技不是唯一答案

## Fact card

### Proposed angle

Use the IWC's 2026 vessel-strike workshop summary to explain why detection technology cannot replace speed reduction, route changes, and shared reporting standards.

### Central claim

Reducing cetacean vessel strikes requires operational measures and usable shared data. Detection tools can support those measures, but they do not keep vessels and whales apart by themselves.

### Confirmed facts

- The workshop took place on 21 April 2026 during the European Cetacean Society conference in Dundee, Scotland.
- The IWC summary says that the workshop heard 40 expert presentations across risk assessment, operational mitigation, detection technology, mariner education, and industry perspectives.
- The summary identifies speed reduction and route adjustment as the most consistently effective mitigation tools discussed at the workshop.
- The summary says that vessel-strike records still contain reporting, standardisation, and interoperability gaps.
- The summary describes passive acoustic monitoring, thermal detection, visual detection, and decision-support tools as supporting measures rather than substitutes for separating vessels from cetaceans.

### Uncertainty and limits

- This package relies on the IWC's workshop summary, not an independent systematic review of every mitigation measure.
- The package does not establish the size or distribution of vessel-strike risk in Taiwan.
- The source does not provide a Taiwan-specific route, speed limit, or policy recommendation.

### Taiwan connection

The connection is contextual. Taiwan has dense maritime activity and recorded cetacean diversity, but this source does not show where Taiwan's collision hotspots are. A Taiwan article should ask whether comparable vessel-traffic, cetacean-distribution, and collision-reporting data can be combined. It must not claim that an international risk map applies to Taiwan.

### Reader action

Read the original workshop summary and look for public Taiwan data on cetacean distribution, vessel traffic, and collision records. Treat the absence of an integrated public map as a research question, not proof that no work exists.

### Claims not to publish

- Taiwan has a confirmed vessel-strike hotspot.
- A specific technology prevents vessel strikes on its own.
- The workshop findings are a binding IWC rule.
- The IWC endorses Khun-Sin or this interpretation.

## 繁體中文草稿

當人們談到船隻撞擊鯨豚，最吸引注意的往往是即時偵測技術。水下聲學、熱影像與視覺辨識，都可能幫助船員更早發現動物。但國際捕鯨委員會在 2026 年發布的工作坊摘要提醒，技術不是讓風險消失的捷徑。

該工作坊整理了 40 場專家發表。主題涵蓋風險評估、航運措施、偵測技術、船員教育與產業觀點。摘要指出，降低航速與調整航線仍是最一致有效的減緩方式。偵測工具的角色，是協助船舶採取這些措施，而不是取代它們。

資料也是問題的一部分。若船撞紀錄、船舶動態與鯨豚分布使用不同格式，或通報不完整，即使蒐集了大量資料，也不一定能轉成可執行的航行決策。因此，保育工作不只需要新感測器，也需要共同的通報標準、可交換的資料與明確的操作規則。

這份資料沒有告訴我們臺灣哪裡是船撞熱點，也沒有提出適用臺灣的航速或航線。對臺灣讀者而言，真正值得追問的是：目前有哪些公開的鯨豚分布、船舶交通與碰撞紀錄？這些資料能否在不暴露敏感位置的前提下，形成可供研究與管理使用的風險圖？

## English adaptation

Detection technology attracts attention in discussions about whale and dolphin vessel strikes. Acoustic monitoring, thermal cameras, and visual systems may help crews detect animals earlier. An International Whaling Commission workshop summary published in 2026 makes a narrower point: detection does not remove collision risk on its own.

The workshop brought together 40 expert presentations on risk assessment, operational measures, detection, mariner education, and industry practice. The IWC summary identifies slower vessel speeds and route adjustments as the most consistently effective measures discussed. Detection systems can support those measures, but they do not replace the physical separation of vessels and cetaceans.

The summary also describes gaps in reporting, standardisation, and data exchange. Collision records, vessel traffic, and cetacean distribution data must work together before managers can identify risk and choose practical measures.

This source does not identify a collision hotspot in Taiwan or prescribe a Taiwanese speed or routing rule. The useful Taiwan question is whether public vessel traffic, cetacean distribution, and collision records can support a local risk assessment without exposing sensitive wildlife locations.
